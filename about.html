ABOUT

Everything online is ranked by something nobody will explain: an algorithm, a follower count, a paid partnership that does not admit to being one. TopTen.one does the opposite. 72 rankings, and the only thing that moves you up is money. Creators and the platforms they are on, crypto, football, fighters, artists, billionaires, politics, cars, cities, pets.

How it works

Pick a ranking, say what you are listing — a profile, a coin, a club, a city, a dog — and pay. Your listing appears at whatever position the money buys, immediately. There is no review, no waiting and no account to create.

Payments are cumulative. A listing's position is the total ever paid towards it, so $20 today and $30 next week is a $50 listing. Anyone can add money to any listing — including someone else's, if they want to push a friend up.

The whole ranking is shown, not a top ten with everybody else hidden underneath. Beside every listing is the figure that would take it past the one above.

What #1 costs

If two listings hold the same total, the one that reached it first stays above. So taking a position costs a cent more than matching it — never the same, and never less. The minimum payment is $2, so where that cent-perfect figure is smaller, the minimum is what it actually costs. On most rankings here, the smallest payment the site takes is also the one that wins.

Listings expire

Thirty days after its last payment a listing drops off. Its total is kept, so a single new payment brings it back exactly where the money says it belongs. Nobody owns a position forever.

Why it is honest

Paid placement is everywhere; almost nowhere is it labelled. Here it is the whole mechanism, printed next to every name. Nothing on this site is ranked by anything other than what people paid, and no figure on it is invented — where the honest number is zero, the page says so.
